About me

I am a first-year Ph.D. student in a joint training programme between BNU–HKBU United International College and Great Bay University. I received my M.Sc. in Biological Engineering from the University of Saskatchewan (2026), where I worked with Prof. Yen-Han Lin on machine learning approaches for redox potential–controlled very-high-gravity ethanol fermentation, and my B.Eng. in Biological Engineering from Northwest A&F University (2024).

My research sits where fermentation bioprocess engineering meets machine learning. I build soft sensors and kinetic models that turn cheap, continuous online signals — oxidation–reduction potential (ORP) in particular — into quantitative, forward-looking estimates of metabolic activity, so that a fermentation can be steered before it fails rather than analysed after it ends.

Research experience

Deep learning monitoring and forecasting system for ethanol fermentation
Jun. 2025 – present
  • Fused sparse offline measurements with dense online ORP signals to build a high-resolution training set; developed a real-time state-estimation model (ORPSN, RNN) and a multi-horizon forecasting model (ORPSC, LSTM encoder–decoder) for metabolic dynamics.
  • Outcome: Achieved high-accuracy soft sensing of ethanol production and glucose consumption rates; maintained accuracy over an 8-hour forecast horizon, providing a basis for proactive feed-forward control.
Bayesian inference for fermentation kinetics and uncertainty analysis
Jan. 2025 – Dec. 2025
  • Built Bayesian hierarchical kinetic models for multiple fermentation systems with algorithmic and literature-informed priors; systematically compared Bayesian and frequentist inference for parameter identifiability and predictive stability under sparse data.
  • Outcome: Resolved non-convergence of conventional methods in small-sample settings, giving robust kinetic parameter estimates with explicitly quantified uncertainty and improved biological interpretability.
Allele-specific chromatin interactions and imprinted gene regulation in maize endosperm
Mar. 2023 – May 2024
  • Applied autoencoders and U-Net to denoise and enhance the resolution of Hi-C data from reciprocal crosses of maize inbred lines B73 and Mo17; constructed allele-specific chromatin interaction maps; partitioned A/B compartments and identified TADs genome-wide; analysed compartment distributions around imprinted genes.
  • Outcome: Built an allele-resolved 3D chromatin architecture of maize endosperm and showed that the compartmental distribution of imprinted genes has no clear parent-of-origin dependence, indicating that chromatin reorganisation at the A/B compartment level is not the principal mechanism of imprinting regulation there.
Metagenomics and culturomics for tobacco biological control
Nov. 2021 – Nov. 2022
  • Screened and validated antagonistic strains and assembled consortia; designed and ran field trials; performed 16S rRNA amplicon analysis of the tobacco rhizosphere microbiome.
  • Outcome: Established a biocontrol strain library with good field efficacy and developed an effective composite microbial agent.
